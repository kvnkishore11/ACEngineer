# Brief: What's the fastest path to a $120K+ software engineering career?

## Situation
I'm 24, working as a barista making $38K/year. I've been self-teaching Python and JavaScript for 6 months — I can build basic CRUD apps and have deployed 2 small projects on Vercel. I want to become a software engineer. I'm evaluating three paths: a 4-year CS degree, a coding bootcamp (12-16 weeks), or continuing self-taught with a structured approach (open-source contributions, building in public, networking). AI is reshaping what junior engineers do — the bar for entry-level is shifting fast.

## Stakes
If I pick the right path and execute well: I'm earning $80-120K within 12-24 months and building a career with compounding upside. If I pick wrong: I waste 1-4 years and $20-80K on a path that doesn't convert to employment, or I enter the market with the wrong skillset as AI changes what companies hire for. The opportunity cost of each year not earning an engineering salary is $80K+.

## Constraints
- Savings: $12,000
- Can work part-time (20 hrs/week) but not full-time while studying intensively
- No family financial support
- Location: can relocate, currently in Austin TX
- Current skills: Python basics, JavaScript/React fundamentals, Git, basic SQL, 2 deployed projects
- No CS network — don't know any engineers personally
- The 2026 junior market is competitive — companies are hiring fewer juniors and expecting more from them
- AI tools (Cursor, Claude, Copilot) are changing what "junior engineer" means — some companies want AI-native developers, others still want fundamentals

## Key Question
Given $12K in savings, 6 months of self-teaching, and a 2026 job market where AI is reshaping entry-level engineering — should I invest in a CS degree, a bootcamp, or double down on self-taught with a deliberate strategy? What's the highest-ROI path to a $120K+ engineering role within 24 months?
