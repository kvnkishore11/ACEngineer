# 2026 Junior Software Engineering Market

## Hiring Reality
- Junior SWE openings are down 35% from 2022 peak
- Companies that are hiring juniors want "AI-native" skills — candidates who use AI tools fluently, not just code from scratch
- The definition of "entry-level" has shifted: companies expect juniors to ship features independently with AI assistance, not just write functions
- Remote junior roles are rare — most companies want juniors onsite for mentorship
- Austin, TX has a strong tech scene: Dell, Oracle, Indeed, Apple, Tesla, plus 200+ startups

## Path Comparison

### 4-Year CS Degree
- Cost: $40-80K (state school) or $10-20K (community college → transfer)
- Time: 4 years
- Employment rate: 72% within 6 months of graduation
- Median starting salary: $78K
- Pros: strong fundamentals, network, internship pipeline, credential for life
- Cons: 4 years of opportunity cost, slow pace, much curriculum isn't practical

### Coding Bootcamp (12-16 weeks)
- Cost: $12-20K (or ISA: 15% of salary for 2 years post-employment)
- Time: 3-4 months full-time
- Employment rate: 40-65% within 6 months (varies wildly by bootcamp)
- Median starting salary: $72K
- Top bootcamps in 2026: Launch School, Recurse Center, App Academy
- Pros: fast, practical, career services, cohort network
- Cons: expensive, inconsistent outcomes, some employers still skeptical, surface-level CS knowledge

### Self-Taught + Structured Strategy
- Cost: $0-2K (courses, hosting, conference tickets)
- Time: 6-18 months depending on intensity
- Employment rate: unknown (no aggregate data)
- Pros: cheapest, most flexible, build real portfolio, AI-native from day one
- Cons: no credential, no structure, no network, easy to get stuck, hard to know when you're "ready"

## What Companies Actually Screen For (2026)
1. Can you ship? (portfolio of deployed projects)
2. Can you work with AI tools effectively? (not just prompting — integrating AI into workflow)
3. Can you debug and reason about systems? (not just write code)
4. Do you have any signal? (open-source contributions, writing, community presence)
5. CS fundamentals (data structures, algorithms) — still asked in interviews but less weighted than before
