# Brief: How do we respond to the FDA warning letter without killing growth?

## Situation
FDA issued a warning letter citing 6 health claims in our Instagram ads and 3 in our blog posts that violate dietary supplement labeling regulations (21 CFR 101.93). Examples: "reduces anxiety," "boosts immunity," "improves sleep quality." Our legal counsel says we have 15 business days to respond with a corrective action plan. This is not a product recall — it's a marketing compliance issue. But 38% of our new subscriber acquisition comes from the Instagram ads that contain these claims.

## Stakes
If we respond aggressively and pull all flagged content immediately: we lose our #1 acquisition channel overnight. CAC will spike, new subscriber growth could drop 40-60% for months while we rebuild compliant creative. If we respond slowly or inadequately: FDA escalates to injunction, product seizure, or consent decree — any of which would be existential. If we get this right: we emerge with a compliant marketing engine that competitors can't easily copy, and "FDA-compliant" becomes a trust signal.

## Constraints
- 15 business days to submit corrective action plan to FDA
- 38% of new subs come from flagged Instagram ads
- Content team: 2 people — already at capacity
- Legal counsel costs: $350/hour, estimate $15-25K for full compliance review
- Competitors are making similar claims and haven't been flagged (yet)
- Our product genuinely helps users — the claims aren't false, they're just not stated in FDA-compliant language
- We cannot make disease claims ("treats," "cures," "prevents") for supplements — only structure/function claims ("supports," "maintains," "promotes")

## Key Question
How do we respond to the FDA warning letter in a way that satisfies compliance within 15 days, minimizes acquisition disruption, and ideally turns this into a competitive advantage?
