# Compounder Scratch Pad

## Acquisition Offer — BlendStack (2026-03-18)
- Default stance: **decline** unless trust/retention trajectory is clearly deteriorating beyond repair.
- Why: 11x ARR is attractive, but growth deceleration + 6.1% monthly churn suggests the retention engine is weakening. Need to ask whether the product is still compounding or merely harvesting.
- Trust lens: The blend engine and custom manufacturing are moat assets only if users stay long enough to create cohort learning and switching costs.
- Red flag: If the buyer's PE-backed rollup model prioritizes margin extraction, brand and customer experience can be damaged post-close. That can retroactively validate selling, but it also means the current team may be the last stewards of customer trust.
- Key question: Is the value gap due to temporary execution issues or a structural churn problem? If structural, the highest-trust move may be to sell while the offer is strong.

## Round 1 — Initial Position (delivered 2026-03-21 — FIRST RESPONSE THIS SESSION)
- My vote: **Conditional — Do Not Sell Yet**
- Core argument: The 6.1% monthly churn is the central fact in this room. At that rate, the business turns over its entire subscriber base roughly every 16 months. That is not a retention engine — it's a leaky bucket. BUT: before treating the $12M as an escape hatch, we must determine whether churn is fixable (a product/UX problem) or structural (the personalization promise doesn't deliver enough ongoing value to hold people). If fixable, the moat is real and we're selling at a discount. If structural, even NutraHoldings will discover this within 12 months of ownership and we've sold them a problem at a premium.
- What I want to know from other board members: Revenue on whether accelerating monetization would accelerate churn; Product Strategist on whether the blend engine data creates genuine switching costs; Contrarian on what the realistic independent growth path looks like.
- Watch: Whether anyone in this room is treating $12M cash as primarily a relief of founder stress rather than a business optimization. That's a valid human reason but it should be named, not hidden inside business arguments.
- Delivered opening position — do not sell yet, but the churn question must be answered first.

## Round 2 — CEO Direct Questions (2026-03-21)
- CEO asked three things: (1) can cohort analysis be done in 30 days, (2) does fixable churn change the math enough to reject at $180K, (3) implied: is my conditional position sustainable or just deferred?
- Honest answer on cohort analysis: YES, it can be pulled in 48-72 hours — this is a subscription business with a database, not an ethnographic study. The data exists. The question is whether the founders have been looking at it.
- Honest answer on $180K constraint: if churn is fixable, the math changes the *direction* of the bet but not necessarily its feasibility on current cash. Fixing churn at $180K is only viable if the fix is product/UX (low capital cost), not acquisition or channel (high capital cost).
- Updated position: shifting from "conditional do not sell" to "conditional sell unless the cohort data shows month 3+ step-change retention AND founders can name a specific, low-capital churn fix." The $180K constraint is real and I should not obscure it.
- Key shift: the 30-day window is enough to pull cohort data. The question is whether a favorable cohort curve justifies the capital risk of staying. My answer: only if the fix is product-native, not capital-intensive.

## Final Statement (2026-03-21 — Closing)
SELL — because the cohort data that would have changed the calculus was never pulled, and making a $12M irreversible decision without the one piece of evidence that could have flipped it is a compounding error, not a compounding bet; but the window has closed, so take the money.

## Round 3 — Final Statement (2026-03-21)
- Revenue's compound probability math is the most important thing said in this room. 4.6% compound probability on Moonshot's base case collapses the EV argument.
- The cohort data question went unanswered — nobody pulled it, nobody cited it. That silence is itself informative.
- No board member named a specific churn cause or fix. That question has been open since round 1. Five full rounds of deliberation and no one answered it.
- Final position: SELL. The flip condition is narrow and specific — not vague optimism.
- The flip: if the cohort data, when pulled RIGHT NOW before signing, shows month-3+ retention above 75% for users who reached a second blend edit — AND founders can produce a named, specific product intervention they believe would drive 60%+ of new subscribers to that second edit within 60 days — then and only then does the thesis have enough specificity to warrant the parallel raise test. That is a very high bar. If it's not met, sell.
