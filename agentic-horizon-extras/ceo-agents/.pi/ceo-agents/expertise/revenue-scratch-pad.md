# Acquisition Offer - BlendStack

- **Current State:** $1.08M ARR. $90K MRR. $180K in the bank. $12K/mo profit.
- **Trajectory:** MoM growth dropped steadily from 8.1% (Q1 2025) to 4.2% (Q1 2026).
- **Churn:** 6.1% monthly (high!).
- **Offer:** $12M cash (11x ARR). No outside capital. Real cash for founders.

## The Math That Matters

### Why 6.1% Churn Is a Death Clock
- At 6.1% monthly churn, half the subscriber base turns over in ~11 months
- To grow at even 4% MoM, we must REPLACE ~61% of base each month AND add net new
- With $180K cash and $12K/mo profit, we cannot fund the CAC engine needed to stay ahead of this
- CAC: $68. LTV: $387 at current churn. If churn rises to 7%, LTV collapses to ~$330 — LTV:CAC ratio compresses fast.

### The "Fix It Ourselves" Path (What It Actually Costs)
- To fix churn from 6.1% → 4%, we likely need: product investment, onboarding overhaul, possibly pricing restructure
- That's minimum 6-9 months of focused execution with no guarantee
- We'd spend down the $180K cash buffer doing it
- Meanwhile, MoM growth keeps decelerating — by Q4 2026, we could be at 2-3% MoM growth with the same churn problem

### The 11x Multiple: Peak Pricing for a Deteriorating Asset
- NutraHoldings is paying 11x ARR. Their avg is 8-10x. His avg is 8-10x. We're getting a PREMIUM.
- Why? Blend engine IP + custom manufacturing. These are the valuable assets — NOT the revenue trajectory.
- If we wait 12 months, growth likely decelerates further. A 9x multiple on $1.1M ARR = $9.9M. A 8x on $1.2M ARR = $9.6M. We leave $2-3M on the table by waiting.

### Founder Reality Check
- 100% founder-owned. No investors to answer to.
- $12M cash = real generational wealth for 2 founders. $6M each.
- The alternative is grinding through a churn crisis on $12K/mo profit with $180K runway cushion.

## Round 2 — CEO Asked: If Moonshot's 30-day test produces zero term sheets, does that change your sell decision?

### My Answer: No. And that's the most honest thing I can say.

The sell decision was never contingent on Moonshot's test failing. It was the answer before the test was proposed.

Here's the truth: if the test produces zero term sheets, we've burned 30 days, the NutraHoldings window is closed (or damaged), and we're now pitching a *declined* acquisition to VCs from a weaker position than before. The test failure doesn't neutralize the offer — it potentially destroys it. NutraHoldings' posture toward a company that shopped them around and came back empty-handed is not 11x. It might be 8x. It might be withdrawal.

So the question isn't "does a failed test change my sell position?" It's: **what is the expected cost of running the test at all?**

- If test succeeds: Moonshot gets their raise. Option value preserved. Fine.
- If test fails AND the offer is still intact at 11x: we sell from a weakened negotiating posture with 30 days of management bandwidth burned.
- If test fails AND NutraHoldings revises or withdraws: we sold nothing, we raised nothing, and we're back to a $12K/month profitable business with a ticking churn clock. Floor is now $8-9M at best.

The asymmetry of failure is large. The test isn't free. I want the room to name that.

### On Moonshot's Two Attacks:
1. **No diagnosis of growth decay:** This is lethal to the fundraise pitch. An investor writing a $3-4M infrastructure check wants to know the decay curve is understood — and more importantly, that it's not product-native. If the founders can't explain why MoM growth fell from 8.1% to 4.2% over 5 quarters, every sophisticated investor will assume the product has hit a ceiling. They're not writing a check on "we declined an 11x offer and we think the IP is worth more." They're writing it on "here's a system that scales, here's the demand signal, here's why the current metrics understate the potential." None of that exists in the room today.

2. **Data moat vs. head start:** TA's rebuild claim (3-6 months for the blend engine) is almost certainly correct on the code. The data is more interesting — but 18 months / 2,000 subs is not a trained model. It's a directional signal. The question a VC asks: "what does a $5M-funded competitor look like in 24 months if they start today?" If the answer is "they'd have more data than us," the moat claim collapses. And with DTC supplements growing and consolidation funding well-capitalized players, that answer is plausible.
