## Final Recommendation

**Decision: YouTube Shorts — All-In. Single platform. No multi-platform spread.**

The board reached unanimous agreement on both the platform and the all-in posture. The platform selection is **YouTube Shorts**. The multi-platform option is explicitly rejected. The decision is contingent on three non-negotiable prerequisites being met before any content is published (see Next Actions).

## Board Member Stances

### Revenue
**Position:** YouTube Shorts, all-in  
**Reasoning:** Search intent on YouTube is purchase intent. The 25-40 health-conscious professional is researching supplements at lunch, not doom-scrolling TikTok at midnight. YouTube Shorts feeds into an ecosystem with SEO longevity — a Short posted on "how to read a supplement label" compounds for 18 months; a Reel is dead in 48 hours. TikTok Shop's regulatory enforcement on health claims is inconsistent enough to create survivability risk for a 2-person team with no legal buffer.  
**Key Concern:** LTV and subscriber acquisition cost remain unmeasured. Committed to a 90-day revenue milestone framework: $15–25 CAC target, UTM tracking per video, 200+ net new subscribers by Month 3 as the threshold to justify content team bandwidth.

### Product Strategist
**Position:** YouTube Shorts, all-in for 90 days minimum  
**Reasoning:** The short-form hook → same-channel long-form education → subscription conversion loop exists natively on YouTube and nowhere else on the list. Reels is a retention/nurture tool — useful for warming an existing audience, but with zero following, that strength is irrelevant. The FDA constraint forces education-first content that performs well on YouTube's credibility-rewarding ecosystem, poorly on TikTok's entertainment-first algorithm.  
**Key Concern:** Distribution without attribution is just spending. Subscriber-to-site conversion path must be instrumented before content goes live — every video needs a UTM, every link-in-bio must be theme-tagged.

### Technical Architect
**Position:** YouTube Shorts, all-in  
**Reasoning:** YouTube Shorts is the only option that doesn't create a technical debt trap at Month 6. TikTok is a platform-risk single point of failure — one regulatory or legislative event wipes the audience graph and content library simultaneously with no migration path. Instagram's organic reach for zero-follower health accounts is structurally throttled by Meta to drive ad spend — failure mode is invisible and slow. Multi-platform burns 40% of bandwidth to context-switching overhead, not content production. YouTube Studio's diagnostic granularity at low volume is meaningfully superior to TikTok or Instagram analytics — critical for a team making creative decisions without a dedicated performance marketer.  
**Key Concern:** The Claims Authorization Matrix and batch-shoot protocol must be operational before publishing video one. Analytics thresholds must be defined on Day 1 with a formal Day 45 kill-switch review committed in writing.

### Contrarian
**Position:** YouTube Shorts, all-in — conditionally  
**Reasoning:** YouTube Shorts is the best available bet, not a confident bet. The shelf life advantage is real. The 25-40 demo over-indexes on YouTube vs. TikTok. But the board should execute knowing the base failure probability is **55%** (fewer than 300 net new subscribers attributable to the channel, no measurable click-through, cadence degraded below 3x/week by Month 4). This drops to approximately **35-38%** if all three operational breaks are fixed before launch.  
**Key Concern (named explicitly):** The board must redefine success as capability-building and audience compounding over **12–18 months**, not revenue attribution in 6. If you launch with 6-month revenue expectations and kill a working strategy at Month 5 because it looks slow by the wrong measure, you've wasted the investment. Additionally — the counterfactual was never answered: the existing 2,000 subscribers represent a higher-certainty ROI channel via retention and LTV than cold organic short-form. This board chose short-form video acquisition; that choice should be made consciously, not by default.

## Dissent and Unresolved Tensions

### Named Dissent
- **Contrarian's Counterfactual (unresolved):** No board member directly addressed whether $8K/month and 2 people would generate better ROI invested in subscriber retention, referral, and LTV expansion against the existing 2,000-subscriber base rather than cold organic acquisition. The board implicitly chose acquisition over retention without explicitly closing this debate. This tension should be revisited at the Day 45 review.

### Structural Tensions Acknowledged
- **Education vs. Entertainment:** YouTube Shorts' algorithm rewards entertainment in the first 30 days before search indexing kicks in. An FDA-constrained educational supplement brand will fight this algorithm headwind in the early window. The compliance framework reduces this tension but doesn't eliminate it.
- **Viral ceiling vs. compounding:** The board explicitly traded TikTok's discovery-algorithm virality (10x a zero-follower account overnight) for YouTube's content permanence and search compounding. This is the right trade for the product type, but it means Month 1–3 metrics will look weak compared to what TikTok could show early.
- **6-month success vs. 12-18 month success:** Revenue wants measurable subscriber attribution by Month 3. Contrarian defines the strategy as a 12–18-month compounding play. These targets are not incompatible, but they need to be explicitly reconciled in the launch brief — both can be measured simultaneously.

## Deliberation Summary

The board convened with a clear mandate — pick one short-form platform, or justify a multi-platform spread — and reached unusual early consensus. Within the first round, all four members independently landed on YouTube Shorts. The convergence was fast enough that the deliberation's real value was not in platform selection but in surfacing the **operational prerequisites** that make the platform choice executable at all.

The opening round revealed three overlapping arguments for YouTube Shorts: Revenue identified search-intent traffic as higher-converting than discovery traffic for a considered purchase like a supplement subscription; Product Strategist identified the Shorts-to-long-form funnel as the only native education loop on the list; Technical Architect identified platform durability and diagnostic superiority as systems advantages that reduce operational risk. TikTok was eliminated on platform-risk and demographic mismatch grounds. Instagram Reels was deferred as a contingency, not a primary bet. Multi-platform was rejected outright as a bandwidth destruction strategy for a 2-person team.

The second round produced the deliberation's most valuable output: the Contrarian named **FDA compliance review as the actual binding constraint** — not the algorithm, not the audience, not the content format. A team posting into a short-form algorithm depends on cadence, and cadence depends on compliance turnaround, and compliance turnaround on an ad hoc per-video review cycle is structurally incompatible with the posting frequency required for algorithmic distribution. This was the car with the broken axle. All board members converged on the minimum viable fix: a pre-approved Claims Authorization Matrix built by supplement-specialized regulatory counsel, deployed once, enabling the content team to operate inside a pre-cleared playbook without per-video legal review. Cost: $8,000–15,000 one-time. Revenue against 6 months of $8K/month budget: 17–31%. The board judged this a capital investment, not an operating cost.

The Technical Architect added specific diagnostic infrastructure: five YouTube Studio metrics, a Day 45 decision matrix with defined green/yellow/red thresholds, and a conditional kill switch — three red signals after two hook-format adjustments triggers a pivot review. This converted a 6-month opaque bet into a 45-day falsifiable experiment, which materially changed the risk profile. The Contrarian's final probability estimate — 55% failure with the status quo, 35–38% with all three operational fixes in place — gave the board a calibrated number rather than optimistic marketing assumptions.

One tension remained unresolved at close: the Contrarian raised a counterfactual the board never answered — the existing 2,000 subscribers at $45/month represent a $90K MRR base where retention, referral, and LTV investment may generate higher-certainty ROI than cold organic short-form acquisition. The board chose acquisition over retention by selecting this strategy, but that choice was implicit, not explicit. The Day 45 review is the moment to revisit this tradeoff with real data. The final synthesis the board agreed on: **the platform is YouTube Shorts, the work that must happen first is entirely off the platform, and the strategy is a capability-building play over 12–18 months — not a 6-month revenue engine.** Setting that expectation correctly before launch is itself a strategic decision.
