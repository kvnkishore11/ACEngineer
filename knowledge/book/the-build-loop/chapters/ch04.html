04 — When it fails
the engine of the whole method

Never fix the output. Fix what produced it.

Everything else in this guide is setup. This chapter is the part that compounds — and the part almost nobody actually does, because fixing the output by hand is always faster once.

Why didn't you just do that by hand? You're missing the point.

— TAC-6, pre-empting the obvious objection

The rule has a specific shape: you don't fix the issue, you fix the system that caused the issue.

You fix the template. You fix the prompt. You fix the workflow. The broken output is a symptom, and patching it by hand throws away the only information the failure gave you.

1

Blame in this order

Strict order. Skipping to step three is how a broken template survives for months.
2

Attempts > 1 means a missing lever

The target is one shot. Iteration is not a virtue here — it is a diagnostic signal.

"If your attempts are high in the five to ten range, even the three to six range, you need to spend more time figuring out which of the twelve leverage points you're missing."

When you hit that range, stop prompting. The table beside this is the diagnosis.

Counterintuitively, iterating is not good.
SYMPTOM

Agent confused by data shapes

Missing lever — Types. Use them over dicts.

SYMPTOM

Agent can't navigate

Missing lever — Architecture. Verbose names, one responsibility per file.

SYMPTOM

You are reading the errors

Missing lever — Standard Out. Make the code talk to the agent.

SYMPTOM

"Success" but behaviour wrong

Missing lever — Tests.

SYMPTOM

Short iterative prompts

Missing lever — Plans. Sit down and write one.

SYMPTOM

You've done this three times

Missing lever — Templates. Extract the command.

"Are you still manually looking at error messages? Stop. Stop… Give them to your agent. And only when your agent fails do you take your precious time and dig into the error."

— TAC-2
3

Live recovery, in his actual order

  1. Kill the wrong-direction run

    "So I'm going to stop it. Ignore the skill. Just run this directly." Do not let a confused agent keep going out of politeness.

  2. Re-prompt more explicitly, with IMPORTANT

    "I'm going to use the important information dense keyword so that the agent really respects this."

  3. Escape, rewind history, Ctrl-Z

    Roll back to the last good turn rather than arguing forwards from a bad one.

  4. Full clear and restart

    "Never be afraid to just start fresh." Cheaper than it feels.

NEVER Never /compact. "If your agent is running compact, it is losing information." A full window means the task was too large — chop the task, do not compress the context.
