# Brief: Do I quit freelancing to go all-in on my Figma plugin?

## Situation
I'm a solo designer/developer. I built a Figma plugin called LayoutForge that auto-generates responsive layouts from rough wireframes. It launched 5 months ago. It's now at $12K MRR and growing 15% month-over-month. I also run a freelance design practice with 3 retainer clients paying $15K/month total. I'm working 70-hour weeks doing both. Something has to give. The plugin needs full-time attention — users are requesting features faster than I can ship, and two competitors just entered the space.

## Stakes
If I drop freelance to go full-time on LayoutForge: I lose $15K/month in guaranteed income, burn my client relationships (they'll replace me), and bet everything on a plugin in a market that's heating up. If it works, I could be doing $50K+ MRR within a year. If it doesn't, I'm starting from zero on both sides. If I keep doing both: I ship slowly, competitors catch up, the growth window closes, and I burn out. If I drop the plugin: I lose a once-in-a-career product-market fit moment.

## Constraints
- Solo operator — no co-founder, no employees, no investors
- Savings: $45,000 (roughly 3 months of living expenses in SF)
- Freelance contracts: 2 clients on monthly retainers (cancellable with 30 days notice), 1 on a 6-month contract ending in 2 months
- Plugin revenue: $12K MRR, 850 paying users, 15% MoM growth
- Plugin churn: 4.2% monthly
- Feature backlog: 47 requests, 3 are "will churn without this" level
- Two competitors launched similar plugins in last 45 days — both VC-backed
- I have zero marketing — growth is 100% organic (Figma community, Twitter, word of mouth)
- Health: I haven't exercised in 3 months, sleeping 5 hours/night

## Key Question
Should I drop my freelance clients and go full-time on LayoutForge, and if so, when and how — or is there a smarter path that doesn't require burning the safety net?
