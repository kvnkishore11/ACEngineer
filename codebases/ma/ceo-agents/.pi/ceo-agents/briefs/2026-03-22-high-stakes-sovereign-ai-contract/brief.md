# Brief: Do we take the $200M sovereign AI contract? (High-Stakes)

## Situation
Our company, Meridian AI, builds enterprise AI deployment infrastructure — we help large organizations run LLMs on-premise with full data sovereignty. We're 85 people, $28M ARR, growing 92% year-over-year. Three weeks ago, a Gulf state sovereign wealth fund (SWF) approached us through their technology investment arm with a proposal: a $200M, 5-year contract to build and operate a national AI infrastructure stack for their country. The contract would make us the exclusive AI infrastructure provider for all government ministries, state-owned enterprises, and the national university system. It would triple our revenue overnight but require us to open a regional HQ, dedicate 60% of our engineering team to the project for 18 months, and comply with data residency and content moderation requirements that conflict with some of our existing enterprise customers' contractual terms.

## Stakes
If we accept: we become a $70M+ ARR company by year 2, we gain a sovereign reference customer that opens every Gulf state and Southeast Asian government contract, and we build infrastructure at a scale no competitor can match. If we decline: we stay on our current growth trajectory (strong but competitive), preserve team focus, and avoid the geopolitical and reputational risks of sovereign AI work. If we half-accept (partial scope): we risk delivering a mediocre product to the most demanding customer type (government) while still losing team focus. The SWF has made it clear: they want a committed partner, not a vendor.

## Constraints
- Contract term: 5 years, $200M total ($30M year 1, $40M years 2-5)
- Exclusivity: Meridian would be the exclusive AI infrastructure provider for the sovereign stack — no other vendors allowed in the core platform
- Team allocation: 50-60 engineers dedicated for first 18 months (we have 52 engineers today — we'd need to hire 30+ in 6 months)
- Regional HQ: required in-country office with at least 15 local hires within 12 months
- Data residency: all data must remain in-country; no cross-border data flows, even for model training
- Content moderation: must implement government-defined content filtering — some categories conflict with our "open deployment" philosophy and 3 enterprise customers' contracts
- Payment terms: quarterly in arrears, 60-day payment cycle, denominated in USD
- Existing customers: 3 enterprise clients (total $4.2M ARR) have contractual "no government surveillance use" clauses that may conflict
- Board: 2 founders + 2 VCs + 1 independent — VCs are enthusiastic, independent board member has concerns about reputational risk
- Current cash: $14M, 20 months runway at current burn

## Key Question
Should Meridian accept the $200M sovereign AI contract — a transformative revenue opportunity that would redefine the company's trajectory — knowing it requires dedicating most of our engineering capacity, navigating geopolitical risk, potentially conflicting with existing customer agreements, and fundamentally changing who we are as a company?
