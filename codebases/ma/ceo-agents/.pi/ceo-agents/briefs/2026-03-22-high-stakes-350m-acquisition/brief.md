# Brief: Do we accept the $350M acquisition or pursue IPO? (High-Stakes)

## Situation
Cloudvault, our enterprise data infrastructure company, has received an unsolicited acquisition offer of $350M from Datadog. We're at $42M ARR growing 68% year-over-year with 340 enterprise customers. We raised a $60M Series C 14 months ago at a $280M post-money valuation — this offer represents a 1.25x markup on that round. Datadog wants our real-time data lineage engine and our Fortune 500 customer base. Our board is split: two VCs want to explore IPO (18-24 months out), one VC prefers the certain exit, and the two founder seats haven't decided. Datadog's CEO flew in personally to make the pitch. They want an answer in 45 days.

## Stakes
If we sell at $350M: founders and early team see meaningful liquidity ($40-60M for each co-founder after dilution), VCs get a 4-5x return (good, not great), and the product gets absorbed into Datadog's observability stack. If we pursue IPO: based on current growth and public market comps, the company could be valued at $800M-$1.2B in an IPO — but we need to maintain 50%+ growth for 6 more quarters, hire a CFO, build out a public company infrastructure, and survive market volatility. If we do nothing: the offer expires, we continue on the VC path, but we've now shown our hand to a strategic competitor who may build or acquire a substitute.

## Constraints
- 45-day decision window (Datadog expects exclusivity during diligence)
- Board composition: 2 VC seats, 2 founder seats, 1 independent — need 3 of 5 to approve
- $18M cash in bank, 22 months runway at current burn ($800K/month)
- Key employee retention: 3 VP-level leaders have verbal "stay" commitments but no signed retention agreements
- Datadog wants the engineering team (120 of our 180 people) to relocate to NYC or remain remote under Datadog management
- Non-solicitation: if deal falls through, Datadog cannot hire our people for 18 months (standard NDA/standstill)
- Customer contracts: 12 Fortune 500 customers on 3-year enterprise agreements, $28M in contracted future revenue
- Two competing products are emerging: one well-funded startup ($40M Series B) and an AWS-native lineage feature in preview

## Key Question
Should we accept $350M from Datadog — a certain exit at a modest premium to our last round — or reject it and pursue an IPO path that could yield 2-3x more but requires 18+ months of execution in an uncertain market with rising competition?
